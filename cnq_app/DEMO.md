# CNQ app — 10-minute demo script

A step-by-step walkthrough for showing the app with the bundled **simulated**
demo data. Everything here is generated by `make_demo.py` (see
[DEVELOPER.md](DEVELOPER.md)); there is no real patient data.

> **Numbers marked `‹…›` are placeholders.** Fill them from a real run:
> `python make_demo.py` prints the model size and training time, and the demo's
> external-validation metrics come from step 4/5 below (or `pytest -q
> tests/test_demo.py -s`). Do not guess them.

**Before you start:** launch the app (`python run.py`), open
<http://127.0.0.1:8000/>, and hard-refresh (Ctrl+Shift+R). The demo model appears
in the Predict tab automatically.

Population (for context, not for the audience): the training data is Weibull
survival times whose *shape* depends on the covariates, ~50% censoring; the
"shifted population" file draws outcomes from a different (Gamma) distribution.
Times are labelled **months**.

---

## 1. Train on the demo data (~‹train time›)

1. On the **Train a model** tab, click **Use demo training data**. The mapping
   fills in: duration = `time`, event = `event`, ID = `subject_id`, unit =
   `months`, and all covariates `x0…x11` selected.
2. Point out the **validation summary**: ~1,500 subjects, ~‹events› events,
   ~‹censoring›% censored. No errors — it's ready to train.
3. Under **Models**, choose **Trans-CNQ** (TransformerPS_gaps) — or KAN-CNQ.
   Set **Quantile levels → Every 5%** (0.05–0.95) so the survival curves show.
   Leave the paper preset, or set a small custom config for speed.
4. Click **Start training** and watch the progress bar (fit N of total, epochs).

**Point out on the results:**
- **Non-crossing quantiles** — in the report's *Individual quantile profiles*,
  each subject's predicted times increase monotonically with τ; the metrics
  table shows an **adjacent crossing rate of 0.00%** (the `_gaps` models can't
  cross by construction).
- **Calibration** — the *Per-τ calibration* plot sits close to the diagonal:
  predicted quantiles match observed coverage.
- **Individual profiles / survival curves** — with the dense grid, each subject
  gets a smooth predicted survival curve S(t) = 1 − τ.

---

## 2. Save a model (~‹refit time›)

On the results, open the **Save a model** panel and explain the three options:

- **Final model: refit on all data** *(recommended)* — retrains the chosen model
  on every row (15% held out for early stopping). Best for reuse.
- **Ensemble of the repeated-split models** — averages each split's model on the
  log scale (needs a run with >1 split).
- **Single split** — one split's model.

Save a **Final model** named e.g. `demo-talk`, watch the refit progress, and note
the **Download ….cnqmodel** link. Mention that only the most recent
`CNQ_KEEP_RUNS` (20) runs are kept, so saving is how you keep a model.

*(The shipped `demo_model.cnqmodel` is exactly a saved Final model — you can skip
saving and just use it in the next step.)*

---

## 3. Predict for 20 new patients

1. Switch to the **Predict new subjects** tab. In **Choose a model**, pick
   **Demo model (simulated data)** — note the **demo** badge and that it has no
   Delete button. Its summary shows the features, quantile levels and training
   size.
2. Under **Upload new subjects → Or use demo new subjects**, click
   **20 new patients (no outcomes)**.
3. The feature columns auto-map (`x0…x11`); the extra **`note` column is
   ignored**. Click **Run prediction**.

**Point out on the results:**
- The **predictions table**: each patient's `median`, the 80% interval
  (`interval80_low`/`high`/`width`) and every `q_τ`.
- **Interval widths differ between patients** — the model predicts a different
  amount of between-subject spread depending on covariates.
- **Exactly 2 subjects are flagged `out_of_range = 1`** (P01 and P02 — their
  `note` says a covariate was pushed outside the training range). Their
  predictions are extrapolations.
- The report adds the **distribution of predicted medians and interval widths**.

---

## 4. External validation on 500 new patients

1. Load a fresh set: **Or use demo new subjects → 500 with outcomes
   (validation)**. Because this file has outcomes, **Time = `time`** and
   **Event = `event`** are pre-selected under *External validation*.
2. Run it. In the results summary and report:
   - **80% interval coverage ≈ ‹coverage_80›%** (close to the nominal 80%),
     **50% coverage ≈ ‹coverage_50›%**;
   - **Uno C-index ≈ ‹uno_c›**, IPCW pinball ≈ ‹pinball›;
   - the **observed (Kaplan–Meier) vs mean-predicted survival** curves track each
     other closely, and per-τ calibration is near the diagonal.

The point: on data from the **same population** the model never saw, it's
well-calibrated.

---

## 5. The shifted population — why validate on your own data

1. Load **Or use demo new subjects → 500 shifted population** and run it (time
   and event are pre-selected again).
2. Compare with step 4:
   - coverage drifts away from nominal (**80% coverage ≈ ‹coverage_80_shift›%**),
   - the observed-vs-predicted survival curves **separate**, and calibration
     bows away from the diagonal.

**The message:** the model is fine on its own population but **miscalibrated when
the population shifts**. Reported metrics from one cohort don't transfer — this is
exactly why you validate on *your* data before trusting predictions.

---

## Wrap-up talking points

- The quantile spread is **between-subject variation**, not the model's own
  uncertainty.
- **Out-of-range** subjects are flagged so you know when you're extrapolating.
- **External validation needs unseen data** from your population; the shifted-set
  demo shows what happens when that assumption breaks.
- Everything ran **locally** — the data never left the machine.
